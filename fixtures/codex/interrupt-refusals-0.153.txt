codex 0.153.4 — the PRE-ADMISSION baseline: what a `turn/interrupt` met, from
every party that had an opinion about it, and what the daemon knows when its
control link's wire dies
==============================================================================

THIS FILE IS A HISTORICAL MEASUREMENT, NOT CURRENT BEHAVIOUR — and it is the
measurement that CAUSED the change. It was captured while the broker's **ccd**
allowlist carried no arm for `turn/interrupt` at all, which is why all six of
its ccd rows read `not permitted for this connection`. That rule was removed on
the strength of these rows: the ccd leg now carries the same gated arm the tui
leg has (`Disposition::InterruptActiveTurn`, `codex-broker/src/allowlist.rs`),
bound to this session's own running turn. Re-running the probe therefore does
not reproduce the ccd rows below, and nothing here may be quoted as what the
ccd leg does. The bytes are kept exactly as they were measured, because a
pre-admission baseline is only evidence for as long as it stays the
pre-admission one.

Three connections were driven against one live session, because the three
questions had three different subjects: the broker's **ccd** leg (the path a
phone's interrupt would take), the broker's **tui** leg (where the broker's own
interrupt rule was already wired), and the app-server's own socket (what codex
does with no broker in between). Every row is one request and the answer it was
given. A subscribed ccd leg was tapped throughout, and the frames it read across
the whole driven lifecycle are `codex/interrupt-0.153.jsonl`.

Written by the live probes in `ccd/src/codex_link_live.rs`
(`CC_CODEX_4A_EVID=<dir>`). Nothing reads this file back: it is a record, not a
pin, so it is never re-derived and cannot go red on its own. It carries no
absolute path and no personal identifier — the thread, turn and request ids are
the app-server's own, and they are the only opaque values in it.
