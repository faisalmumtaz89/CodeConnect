# CodeConnect Privacy Policy

**Effective date: November 23, 2026**

CodeConnect is an iPhone app and a Mac app that connect to the CodeConnect daemon (`ccd`) running on **your own computer**. This policy covers all of it, the apps and the software on your computer (a Mac or a Linux computer), because they are one system.

There is no CodeConnect account, no CodeConnect login and no analytics. CodeConnect moves your data between your own devices. The deliberate exceptions: a push notification passes through Apple, and — unless your daemon holds its own Apple push key — through CodeConnect's push relay first. Besides its website, codeconnect.sh, the relay is the one service CodeConnect operates; it exists so notifications can reach a closed app, and it is designed to be incapable of receiving your content. The coding agents you use with CodeConnect are separate: each sends your conversation to the AI provider it uses, as described under *Every network connection, enumerated*.

## What we collect

**Nothing about your work.** The push relay receives only what is listed under *The push relay* below; its interface has no field for project names, session identifiers, commands, file paths, diffs, or conversation content. Neither app contains analytics, advertising, crash-reporting, or tracking SDKs of any kind. There is no account to create, so there is no identity to store.

## Where your data lives

- **On your computer** — the daemon keeps its state under `~/.codeconnect`: the event log of your sessions and your agents' messages to each other (a local SQLite database), pairing records for your devices, each with the Tailscale device it signed in from when it has one, TLS material, its logs, configuration, and, for each Claude Code or Codex account you add, a name the app gives it, such as “Claude Code 2”, and a folder where that agent keeps its sign-in to the account, as it keeps your own sign-in in your home folder (Claude Code on a Mac keeps the credential itself in the Mac's keychain). CodeConnect does not read these sign-ins. CodeConnect transmits none of it anywhere except directly to your paired devices and, for your agents' messages, to your own other computers, as listed under *Every network connection, enumerated*. Notifications are the exceptions, exactly as described under *Push notifications*: in direct mode the computer sends Apple the notification itself, whose title can be a project's folder name; relay-backed, it sends the relay only the fields listed under *The push relay*.
- **On your iPhone** — session data is cached locally solely so the app works offline and resumes quickly. Like the app's other files, but not its Keychain items, this cache is part of your iPhone's backups, including iCloud Backup when it is on. The pairing — your computer's address and the per-device token — is stored in the iOS Keychain. So is a record of each computer you remove (its addresses and identifier), so it isn't added back by itself. When relay-backed notifications are enabled, the relay credential and its generation number, the App Attest key ID, the push token and its environment, and an install identifier are stored there too. All of these are `ThisDeviceOnly` items that never sync to iCloud and never migrate to another phone.
- **On your Mac, with the Mac app**: the app keeps its pairings (each computer's address and this app's token) in the Mac's keychain, and caches session data in `~/Library/Application Support/CodeConnect` so it opens quickly. It installs CodeConnect's binaries and their licence notices in `~/.codeconnect/bin`, and adds one line to `~/.zprofile` only if you choose Set up. Its preferences (`~/Library/Preferences/com.codeconnect.mac.plist`) hold a random identifier for this copy of the app, how far you have read each session, fingerprints of recent Codex messages, so none is sent twice, and your display choices, such as how the list is grouped. When it cannot set up CodeConnect, it writes why to the Mac's own system log.
- **On the push relay** — once a phone has enrolled for relay-backed notifications, the content-free records listed under *The push relay*, for the periods given there.

## What the app does with data, on your devices

- **Session data** (agent transcripts, permission prompts, diffs) travels directly from the daemon on your computer to your iPhone, and to the Mac app, over a connection you configure, typically your own private network. It never passes through servers we operate.
- **Pairing** needs no code for a phone or computer signed in to the same Tailscale account as your computer, when neither of them is a tagged device and your computer does not run Tailscale in userspace mode: your computer asks Tailscale, on the computer itself, which device is connecting, and gives that device its own revocable token. No CodeConnect server takes part. Any other device pairs with a code, shown on your computer as a QR code your iPhone camera reads, or typed by hand. The QR code is a link to `codeconnect.sh/p` with the code after its `#`, the part of a link a browser never sends: if a browser opens it, the page offers the App Store, and codeconnect.sh, hosted on Vercel, receives an ordinary page visit (your IP address and browser) without the code. The code itself is processed on-device and never uploaded to a CodeConnect server; your computer stores only a hash of it. A computer signed in to your Tailscale is trusted as a whole: every user account on it can reach your sessions. For a shared computer, use another Tailscale account.
- **Dictation** in the compose bar uses Apple's speech recognition, on-device wherever your language supports it. Audio is transcribed while you dictate and is not recorded or kept.
- **Revoking a device**: you can revoke a device at any time on each computer it uses, with Block in the Mac app's Settings or with `codeconnect revoke`. Revocation takes effect on that computer immediately, including on open connections, and covers every other device it recognized through Tailscale as the same phone or computer. It stays out, across restarts too, until you let it back in with no new code (Allow Again in the Mac app, or `codeconnect unblock`) or pair it again with a code, and until you allow it again, a computer revoked this way can no longer exchange your agents' messages with this one.

## Push notifications

When enabled, notifications reach your phone one of two ways, and only while an approval or a question is waiting that you can answer from the phone. In both, nothing an agent wrote, ran, or changed is ever included: no command text, no file paths, no diffs, no conversation content.

- **Direct**, when your daemon holds its own Apple push key: your computer talks straight to Apple's push service. For a single run, the alert's title is the **project** the decision is in — the last component of its working directory, for example `Aion`, or `CodeConnect` when there is no unambiguous label — and the body is one of two canned sentences (“Waiting on an approval”, “Asking you a question”). When more than one decision is waiting, the body is `{n} waiting for you`; when they belong to more than one run, the project label is dropped and the title is `CodeConnect`. One word says where a tap should go, and it is always `approval`. A test you explicitly request uses the fixed CodeConnect test title and body and a `codeconnect_test` marker. We never see any of it.
- **Relay-backed**, the default: your daemon sends the push relay the notification fields below, and the relay builds a generic notification (titled “CodeConnect”) and forwards it to Apple.

## The push relay

**What it receives.** From your computer, to deliver a notification: the APNs token and its environment, an opaque token-bound credential, a fixed event kind (whether the notification is about an approval or a question), a count of decisions waiting, a test marker when you ask for a test, and ordinary network metadata. From the app, to manage that credential: asking for an enrollment challenge sends nothing but a format version; enrolling and re-enrolling send Apple's App Attest attestation — carrying the key ID, challenge, token, and environment — that a genuine copy of the app is asking; rebinding and rotating send an App Attest assertion over the binding; a status check is a read-only GET carrying only the bearer credential. Each of these requests also carries ordinary network metadata.

**What it keeps.** Content-free relay state and short-lived operational records: the internal row relationships, a hash of the App Attest key ID, the attestation environment, the verified public key and receipt, an assertion counter and its trust state, the attested bundle version, the validation category, the APNs environment, the credential generation, hashes of the APNs token and bearer credential (never their raw values), the binding status and any terminal reason, timestamps, expiring challenge hashes, and opaque rate-limit buckets. There is no account, but the record is linked at the device level: the push token is a device identifier, and the enrollment record is joined to it rather than to a name.

**How long.** Retention is fixed per field: enrollment challenges live at most ten minutes, rate-limit buckets until the end of the day they are counted, and redacted diagnostic logs seven days. The active enrollment record is never age-pruned while it is active. It becomes terminal when the credential is replaced or rotated (resetting notification registration in the app does this, by enrolling afresh), rebound to a changed token, or marked unregistered when Apple reports the push token gone. A terminal record is deleted from the live relay database within 30 days, and the last encrypted backup containing it may remain for up to 31 additional days. Turning relay-backed notifications off on a computer stops that computer using the credential but does not by itself delete the record. The relay's daily backups are encrypted full copies of its database, each removed at the first daily backup after it is 30 days old, so any record above may also remain in a backup for up to 31 days after it leaves the live database.

## Every network connection, enumerated

A privacy policy that says “we respect your privacy” is weaker than one that lists the connections. Here is the complete list of CodeConnect's connections, followed by what your coding agents connect to.

The **iPhone app** connects to:

1. **Your own computer** — directly, over the connection you configure.
2. **Apple** — to register for and receive push notifications, to perform App Attest during enrollment and re-enrollment, on iOS 26 and later to download the on-device speech model for your language the first time you dictate in it, and for speech recognition where your language is not handled on-device. These are Apple's services under Apple's policy.
3. **CodeConnect's push relay** — only to enroll for relay-backed notifications, to check its credential status (daily-debounced, on returning to the foreground or after an eligible relay-mode handshake), and for credential changes afterwards (rotation or rebinding), each carrying only what *The push relay* lists for that request from the app, and nothing else, ever.

The **Mac app** connects to:

1. **Your computers running CodeConnect**, this Mac's own included: directly, the way the iPhone app does, each with this app's own revocable token. That covers the computers you add and the other computers of your Tailscale account that one of them lists. It receives what your agents are doing and, for each Claude Code or Codex account on that computer, its name and the email, plan and usage its agent reports, and starts an agent when you ask, with the folder, agent, model and any first message you choose. Over that same connection, it sends to the computer an agent runs on: your answers to the agent's requests and questions (with any reason you give for a Deny), your messages to it, and which account to add, remove or use. Answering on the Mac asks for no Touch ID or password.
2. **codeconnect.sh, for updates**: about once a day, and when you choose Check for Updates…, the app's updater (Sparkle) fetches `codeconnect.sh/mac/appcast.xml`. codeconnect.sh, hosted on Vercel, receives your IP address and ordinary HTTP request metadata, which names the app's and Sparkle's versions. Sparkle's optional system profile is off, so nothing else about your Mac is sent. An update you accept downloads from CodeConnect's GitHub release, and GitHub receives the same.

The Mac app also asks its own background service on the Mac, through a file (`~/.codeconnect/ccd.sock`), where it can be reached, for pairing codes, and for this computer's devices, to block or allow them again. Its links to codeconnect.sh and tailscale.com open in your browser, only when you click them. It never uses the push relay or Apple's push service.

The **daemon and tools on your computer** connect to:

1. **Your paired devices**: directly. A paired device is also sent, so it can list your computers: whether Tailscale is running on this computer, its Tailscale login, whether it is a tagged device, and each of your other computers it can reach, by name, full MagicDNS name (or Tailscale IPv4 address when it has none) and port. It is also sent, for each Claude Code or Codex account on this computer, its name and the email, plan and usage its agent reports; the computer and the apps keep the name with the conversations that used it, but hold the email, plan and usage only in memory.
2. **GitHub**, only when you run `codeconnect update`: it asks GitHub’s public Releases API for the latest release and downloads that release’s files from GitHub. GitHub receives your IP address and ordinary HTTP request metadata, and CodeConnect sends no session, prompt, file or project data.
3. **Apple** — only when your daemon holds its own Apple push key (direct mode), with the content described under *Push notifications* and nothing else.
4. **CodeConnect's push relay** — only when relay-backed notifications are enabled, and only with the fields listed under *The push relay*. Sessions, approvals, and everything else in this system never touch it.
5. **Your other computers running CodeConnect**: only for your agents' messages to each other, CodeConnect's Mail channel, over Tailscale, and only to Mac and Linux computers of the same Tailscale user that are not tagged, on the daemon's own port. A connection carries one thing: the list of running sessions (each one's address, label, whether it is Claude Code or Codex, and its project, the last component of its working directory), one message with its text, or where a message stands. The receiving computer keeps an unread message for a day at most, and deletes a message that is no longer waiting a week after it arrived, once the computer that sent it has been told what became of it.
6. **Tailscale, for a certificate**: where your tailnet has HTTPS certificates turned on, the daemon asks Tailscale for a publicly trusted certificate in this computer's Tailscale name. Like every such certificate, its name is published in public certificate logs.

The **CodeConnect push relay** itself connects to:

1. **Apple's push service (APNs)** — to deliver the generic `CodeConnect` notification it built from those fields.
2. **Operator-controlled backup storage** — an S3-compatible object store that receives the relay's encrypted daily database backups, each removed at the first daily backup after it is 30 days old. The backup is encrypted before it leaves the relay and contains only the content-free records listed above.

A pairing link opened in a browser loads `codeconnect.sh/p`, as described under *Pairing*; the code in the link never reaches it.

The **coding agents you run**, Claude Code and Codex, are not part of CodeConnect. To do their work, they send your conversation, including the code and file contents in it, to the AI provider they use: normally Anthropic for Claude Code and OpenAI for Codex, through your own account with them. That provider's terms and privacy policy for your kind of account apply, not this policy:

1. **Claude Code** connects to Anthropic. It can also send its own usage figures and error reports to Anthropic and to services Anthropic uses. Anthropic describes what Claude Code sends and what Anthropic keeps in [Claude Code's data usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage) and its [privacy policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy).
2. **Codex** connects to OpenAI. In our short tests of Codex 0.167.0 in November 2026, signed in to ChatGPT, it sent your conversation, its requests for its lists of models and plugins, and its own usage reports to chatgpt.com. In a separate test, not signed in, its terminal window also connected to GitHub for OpenAI's tips, and to GitHub and npm to check for a newer version of Codex. Not signed in, it also tried api.openai.com and downloaded OpenAI's collection of plugins from GitHub. OpenAI's [privacy policy](https://openai.com/policies/row-privacy-policy/) links the version for where you live.

CodeConnect starts these agents and passes them what you send from the app, but it never sits between an agent and its provider: each agent makes its own connection, and CodeConnect neither routes it nor reads it. CodeConnect also gives each agent tools for messaging your other agents, and what those tools return becomes part of the agent's conversation too: the list of your other running sessions (each one's computer, label, agent and project), the messages themselves, and instructions for handling them. So does a fixed line that tells the agent a message is waiting.

CodeConnect connects to no other endpoints. If you choose to reach your computer through a VPN or tunnel you operate (for example Tailscale), that traffic is governed by that provider's policy — CodeConnect does not require any specific provider.

## How the connection is protected

The link between your phone and your computer is TLS (`wss://`), your computer's loopback, or your own private tailnet address. A cleartext local-network mode exists but only behind an explicit configuration flag you set yourself — nothing falls back to it silently. Each device holds its own revocable token, and releases are signed — the Mac binaries with Developer ID, a Linux release's checksums with CodeConnect's release key — with the signature verified by the installer and by `codeconnect update` before anything is installed. The Mac app is signed with Developer ID and notarized by Apple, and its updater installs an update only when the update's signature checks out.

## Deleting your data, and your rights

Privacy laws give you rights to access, correct, export, and delete personal data an organization holds about you.

- **On your devices** — revoke a device with `codeconnect revoke`, remove the daemon with `codeconnect daemon uninstall` and delete `~/.codeconnect`, and in the app's Settings, open each computer under Computers and choose *Remove this computer*, which deletes its pairing and cache, before deleting the app from your phone. Deleting `~/.codeconnect` also deletes the sign-ins of any Claude Code or Codex accounts you added, except, on a Mac, Claude Code's credential for each, which stays in the Mac's keychain until you remove the account in the Mac app, which signs it out, or delete it there. Deleting the app removes its container and cache, but iOS keeps the app's `ThisDeviceOnly` Keychain items — the pairing, the record of computers you removed, and the relay credential with its key ID, push token and install identifier — and they can outlive an uninstall; no shipped action currently leaves every local credential erased — resetting notification registration replaces the relay items and immediately re-enrolls.
- **With the Mac app**: block a device in its Settings, and on each other computer it uses; to remove the app, quit it, move it to the Trash and delete `~/Library/Application Support/CodeConnect` and `~/Library/Preferences/com.codeconnect.mac.plist`. Its pairings stay in the Mac's keychain until you delete them there.
- **On the push relay** — CodeConnect holds only the records listed under *The push relay*. Resetting notification registration in the app enrolls afresh, which supersedes the previous record and starts its deletion clock: 30 days in the live database, and up to 31 more in the last backup. There is no separate on-request deletion path, because the record holds nothing that identifies you beyond the push token it is keyed to.

We hold no copy of your sessions, code, or conversations, so there is none with us to ask about: we never had one. The app's cache of them is part of your iPhone's backups (iCloud Backup, if you use it), under your direct control. What the providers your coding agents use keep is up to their terms for your kind of account, so ask them about any copies they hold. If you believe we have this wrong, contact us and we will answer plainly.
